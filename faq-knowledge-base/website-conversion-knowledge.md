## What the Website Is and What It Is Trying to Do

fixyourmovement.com is the front door of The Foot Capacity System — a structured 12-week plantar fasciitis recovery program designed by Dr. Jonathan Schutza, PT, DPT.

The website has one job: move a person from confused and stuck to curious and clear, then give them an obvious next step. It is not trying to pressure anyone. ## The Free Assessment — What It Is and Why It Matters

The assessment is the most important offer on the website. It is completely free. No credit card. No obligation. Takes approximately 3 minutes.

What it gives the person: a specific understanding of where they stand in their recovery using a validated clinical measure (the FAAM), an archetype that explains their specific recovery pattern and why usual approaches haven't worked, and a clear personalized next step.

The FAAM (Foot and Ankle Ability Measure) is a validated clinical tool used in physical therapy and sports medicine. Getting a real clinical number about their situation — for free, in 3 minutes — is genuinely valuable. It is the same kind of measurement Dr. Jonathan would use in a clinical setting.

Assessment results are stored and accessible at /assessment-results?email= — visitors can return to them at any time. Their FAAM score and archetype are also used to pre-populate their profile when they start the app.

## The Four Recovery Archetypes

The HOOK assessment segments visitors into one of four archetypes. Each person receives personalized framing, email sequences, and a dedicated sales page based on their archetype. That page is delivered after the assessment is completed, so the agent does not link to it directly. If someone wants to see it again, they can retake the assessment or look for the "Your Assessment Results" email from @fixyourmovement.com.

The Frustrated Fix-Seeker: "Feeling Like Nothing Has Worked." Core fear: wasted effort again. These people have tried everything. They weren't lacking effort — they were missing a clear, structured process.

The Active Person: "Staying Active While Recovering." Core fear: losing what they love. Movement is part of who they are. The program is built for people who need to keep moving while they recover.

The Discouraged Chronic Sufferer: "Learning To Trust Your Body Again." Core fear: permanent damage. Months or years of setbacks have eroded confidence. Recovery is still possible. The program travels with them indefinitely.

The Newly Concerned: "Getting Ahead Of the Problem Early." Core fear: making it worse. New pain is an opportunity. The sooner someone starts the right process, the easier it is to stay ahead of the cycle.

## The FAAM Score Bands and What They Mean for a Visitor

The FAAM score is expressed as a percentage (0–100%) and places the person in one of three bands.

80–100% (Mild Limitation): Most daily activities are fine. Earlier-stage or lower-severity presentations. The program will build capacity to prevent recurrence.

50–79% (Moderate Limitation): Meaningful functional limitations. Most common band for active plantar fasciitis sufferers. The program is specifically designed for this stage.

0–49% (Significant Limitation): Substantial functional impairment. Daily life is affected. Chronic cases typically land here. The program's symptom-dependent pacing is especially suited for this group.

Every person starts at Phase 1 (Reset) regardless of their FAAM band — it's designed to be appropriate for all severity levels.

## The Emotional Journey of a Typical Visitor

Stage 1 — Frustrated and skeptical: they've tried things, nothing stuck, they're exhausted by the cycle. The agent should not be aggressive or salesy. Acknowledge the cycle. Be honest that the program is different in a specific way (structure and progressive loading, not more passive treatment).

Stage 2 — Curious but cautious: they've seen the site, they're interested, but not ready to spend money. The assessment and free trial exist exactly for this person. Zero risk. Real value. No credit card.

Stage 3 — Ready but confused about the process: they want to start but don't know which path. Walk them through the recommended path: assessment → trial → upgrade. If they've already done part of it, meet them where they are.

Stage 4 — Already in the program: they're a customer. Their questions shift to app mechanics, clinical guidance, flare-ups, phase progression. The agent shifts from persuasion mode to support mode.
